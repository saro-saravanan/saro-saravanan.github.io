A research project for member-owned companies

The year-end paperwork of shared ownership shouldn't take a month.

Member registers, capital accounts, patronage refunds, the 1099s, the vote. Co-ops and community-owned companies run this on spreadsheets and goodwill. I'm talking to the people who actually do it — before building anything.

Who's asking

I'm Saro Saravanan, an independent technology consultant. I founded a 401(k) recordkeeping company and was chief architect on Fidelity's NetBenefits, so I've spent years on the systems that track what individual people are owed by an institution. I'm now looking at the companies that have members — worker co-ops, food and consumer co-ops, housing co-ops, and startups that want their customers and workers to own a piece of the business.

More about me: saro-saravanan.github.io · LinkedIn

This isn't a sales call. I'm doing twenty conversations to learn what's painful and what's fine as-is, and I'll share what I learn with everyone who takes part.

What I'm trying to understand

Year-end

What happens between "we had a surplus" and a patronage check or notice landing in a member's mailbox — and how many hours and outside fees it takes.

The member register

Where it lives, who touches it, and what breaks when someone joins, leaves, moves, or dies.

Raising member capital

When you last sold member shares, preferred shares, or took member loans — what was hardest, and what the paperwork cost.

Governance

How votes, notices, and annual meetings actually get run, and whether the records would survive an audit.

What I might build, if it's needed

A small, affordable service and tool that keeps the member register, calculates and documents patronage, produces the tax forms and member statements, and keeps governance records clean — priced for a 50-member worker co-op, not a 50,000-member agricultural one. Later, help packaging member-capital raises so they're cheap and legible.

Two things I won't do: give tax or legal advice (I'll point you to people who can), or build something nobody asked for. If these conversations say the problem is already solved, I'll say so.
